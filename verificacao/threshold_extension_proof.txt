FULL DYADIC-SCALE CLASSIFICATION: PROVED CONSEQUENCE OF THE SUBMITTED RESULTS

Notation: D(N)=sum_{k=0}^N 2^k binom(N,k)^2. Let b be an integer,
u a positive odd integer, n>=0, and assume b+u*2^n>=0. Set
t=v2(b(b+1)), using t=+infinity if b=0 or b=-1.

Then the proposed strengthened statement is

v2(D(b+u*2^(n+1))-D(b+u*2^n)) =
    1                        if n=0;
    2                        if n=1;
    n+2*min(n,t)              if n>=2.

This is a derivation, conditional only on the correctness of the submitted
main theorem and basepoint valuation proposition, not a new unproved
generalization inferred from numerical data.

PROOF FOR n>=2:
If t<=n, the submitted main theorem applies verbatim, giving n+2t.
If t>n (including t=infinity), one of b,b+1 is divisible by 2^(n+1).

If b is even, write N=b+u*2^n and M=b+u*2^(n+1). Then v2(N)=n
and v2(M)>=n+1. Both indices are positive in this case. The submitted
basepoint proposition gives v2(D(N)-1)=3n and
v2(D(M)-1)>=3n+3. Their difference therefore has valuation 3n,
which is n+2*min(n,t).

If b is odd, use N+1 and M+1 instead: their valuations are n and
at least n+1. The minus-one basepoint formula gives
v2(D(N)+1)=3n and v2(D(M)+1)>=3n+3. Again subtraction has
valuation 3n. This also treats b=-1; the even case treats b=0.

PROOF OF THE LOW SCALES:
For every nonnegative integer j, terms with k>=3 in the binomial sum
are divisible by 8. Thus

D(j) = 1+2*j^2+j^2*(j-1)^2 (mod 8).

Subtracting 1+2*j leaves
j*(j-1)*(j*(j-1)+2)=4*A*(A+1), A=j*(j-1)/2,
which is divisible by 8. Therefore D(j)=2*j+1 (mod 8).
Consequently D(M)-D(N)=2*(M-N)=u*2^(n+1) (mod 8).
This has valuation 1 for n=0 and valuation 2 for n=1.

SHARPNESS OF THE CURRENT LINEAR BRANCH:
For b=2^t, u=1, n=t-1 with t>=3, the saturated result gives
3t-3, whereas extending the old n+2t expression down one scale
would predict 3t-1. Thus the old linear formula cannot uniformly
start at n=t-1. The correct improvement is adding a saturated branch,
not simply reducing the threshold while retaining that expression.
Example: b=16,u=1,n=2 gives v2(D(24)-D(20))=6, not 10.
For the lower floor n>=2, b=u=1,n=1 gives v2(D(5)-D(3))=2,
not the linear value 3.

FINITE CROSS-CHECK:
The adjacent script checked 148,854 admissible triples, with
-1000<=b<=1000, positive odd 1<=u<=31, 0<=n<=9, and indices
at most 3000. There were no counterexamples. The computation uses
exact integer recurrence values, not floating-point approximations.
These finite checks corroborate the displayed proof; they do not replace it.

EDITORIAL AND MATHEMATICAL VALUE:
This is a clean, concrete strengthening: it covers every dyadic scale,
absorbs b=0,-1 into a unified convention, and explains exactly why the
linear valuation law stops working below the submitted threshold.
It requires no new difficult analytic estimate. Consequently it improves
completeness and presentation, while its conceptual depth is modest:
it is a short corollary of results already proved in the manuscript.
Novelty relative to the external literature has not been established here.
