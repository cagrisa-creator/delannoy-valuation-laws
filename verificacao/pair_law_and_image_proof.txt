EXPLORATORY CONSEQUENCES OF THE SUBMITTED DELANNOY MANUSCRIPT
16 November 2026

Status: deductions from the stated results of the submitted manuscript, not a
separate audit of those results and not a certification of literature priority.
No submitted or public manuscript has been edited.

1. COMPLETE TWO-POINT VALUATION LAW

Let M,N be distinct nonnegative integers, k=v_2(M-N), and
t_X=v_2(X(X+1)), with t_0=+infinity. Then

  v_2(D(M)-D(N)) = k+1                         if k<=1;
                       k+2 min(t_M,t_N)        if k>=2.

Proof. The local coefficient bound in the manuscript gives
F(4u+r)=F(r) mod16, because every nonconstant coefficient of G_r has
valuation at least4. The four constants D(0),...,D(3) are 1,3,13,63,
respectively. Thus the residue values modulo16 are 1,3,13,15.
Different parity of M,N gives valuation1. The same parity with different
residues modulo4 gives valuation2. These settle k=0 and k=1.

Suppose k>=2, so M,N have the same residue modulo4. First suppose
t_M=t_N=t. If both are even, write M=2^t m and N=2^t n with m,n odd;
if both are odd, write M+1=2^t m and N+1=2^t n with m,n odd. In either
case k>=t+1. Exchange M,N so M>N. Put h=M-N=U2^k with U odd positive,
and B=2N-M. Then B+h=N, B+2h=M, v_2(B(B+1))=t and B is neither 0
nor -1. The manuscript's Theorem1 applies and yields k+2t.

Now suppose t_M!=t_N and set a=min(t_M,t_N). Equality of the residues
modulo4 implies a>=2, and unequal valuations of the even quantities
M,N (or M+1,N+1) give k=a. If M,N are even, the basepoint law gives
v_2(D(M)-1)=3t_M and v_2(D(N)-1)=3t_N, including D(0)-1=0 with
infinite valuation. Subtract these terms with unequal valuations to get
3a=k+2a. If M,N are odd, use D(M)+1,D(N)+1 and the analogous
basepoint law. This proves the formula.

Exact computational corroboration: all 524,800 unordered pairs
0<=N<M<=1024 satisfy the formula. Reproduction: pairs_complete_check.py.
This finite check is not used in the preceding proof.

2. EXTENSION TO Z_2 AND CRITICAL POINTS

The same formula holds for all distinct x,y in Z_2, with
t_x=v_2(x(x+1)) and t_0=t_{-1}=+infinity. Approximate x,y by
nonnegative integers. Their difference valuation k stabilizes. The
minimum of the two t-values also stabilizes unless both x,y are
critical points; that exceptional pair is {0,-1}, already covered by
k=0 and F(0)-F(-1)=2. Continuity then preserves the valuation of the
nonzero differences. This proves the extension.

The formula makes F injective on Z_2: distinct arguments always have
a finite difference valuation. For x different from 0,-1 and
t=v_2(x(x+1)), take h tending to 0 with v_2(h)>t. Both t_x and t_{x+h}
equal t. Consequently

  v_2((F(x+h)-F(x))/h)=2t.

All these quotients belong to the closed set 2^(2t) Z_2^times.
Local analyticity gives v_2(F'(x))=2t in the limit. At 0 and -1, the
basepoint law gives v_2((F(x+h)-F(x))/h)=2v_2(h) for small nonzero h,
which tends to infinity; hence F'(0)=F'(-1)=0. There are exactly two
critical points. The basepoint laws and local analyticity show cubic
vanishing of F(x)-1 at 0 and F(x)+1 at -1: their first nonzero Taylor
coefficient has degree3 and is a unit.

3. EXACT IMAGE AND HAAR MEASURE

Notation: Z_2^times is the set of odd 2-adic integers. Normalize additive
Haar measure by mu(Z_2)=1. Then

 F(Z_2) = {1,-1} union (3+16Z_2) union (13+16Z_2)
          union over a>=2 of
          [(1+2^(3a) Z_2^times) union (-1+2^(3a) Z_2^times)].

Each expression 1+2^(3a) Z_2^times is the ball
1+2^(3a)+2^(3a+1) Z_2. The minus-one branch has the corresponding
description -1+2^(3a)+2^(3a+1) Z_2.

Proof of surjectivity on each component. A distance-preserving map
g: Z_2 -> Z_2 is onto. For every m>=1, its induced map modulo 2^m is
well-defined and injective, hence a permutation. For a prescribed
target z, the compact nonempty sets {u:g(u)=z mod2^m} are nested;
their intersection gives a preimage of z. The same argument on the
finite odd residue classes proves that an isometry
Z_2^times -> Z_2^times is onto.

For r=1,2, let

  g_r(u)=(F(4u+r)-F(r))/16.

The complete pair law gives
v_2(g_r(u)-g_r(v))=v_2(u-v), so g_r is an isometry Z_2->Z_2.
Here F(1)=3 and F(2)=13. Thus the two residue-class images are exactly
3+16Z_2 and 13+16Z_2.

For a>=2, define on odd u

  h_a(u)=(F(2^a u)-1)/2^(3a).

The basepoint law makes h_a(u) odd. The pair formula gives
v_2(h_a(u)-h_a(v))=v_2(u-v), so h_a is an isometry of the units
onto themselves. Therefore the image of the shell v_2(x)=a is exactly
1+2^(3a)Z_2^times. Reflection F(-x-1)=-F(x) gives the corresponding
shell near -1. The remaining points 0,-1 map to 1,-1.

These image components are disjoint. The two large balls are 3 and 13
mod16, while the branches near1 and -1 are1 and15 mod16. Within
each branch, distinct a have distinct valuations relative to the
branch center; the center itself is excluded from all shells.

The singleton centers have measure 0. Each of the two large balls has
measure 1/16. A shell-image ball at exponent a has measure 2^(-3a-1).
Consequently

  mu(F(Z_2))=2/16+2 sum_{a>=2}2^(-3a-1)
            =1/8+sum_{a>=2}2^(-3a)
            =1/8+1/56=1/7.

4. SIGNIFICANCE AND LIMITS

The complete pair law broadens the announced result from equal-
valuation even indices to all distinct indices. The exact image and
critical-point statements turn divisibility formulas into a description
of the interpolated function. They require no new computational
assumption beyond the manuscript's established estimates. Their proofs
still depend on the correctness of those estimates. Literature priority
must be checked before claiming these consequences as new. Such a
reorganization adds conceptual scope; it does not turn an elementary
deduction into an independent new proof technique.
